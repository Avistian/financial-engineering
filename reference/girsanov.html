Risk-Neutral Pricing & the Girsanov Theorem

Reference · print-friendly cheat sheet · Units 015–020 (Year 1 Q2)

Why a price is a hedging cost, and how re-weighting the probabilities turns that cost into an average. Taught in Lesson 015; source Shreve II Ch. 5. Builds on Lesson 014 (the solved GBM) and Lesson 011 (martingales, conditional expectation).

The one-period binomial (where everything is visible)

Worked numbers: S0=100, u=1.1, d=0.9, K=100. Zero rates ⇒ Δ=0.5, B=−45, price 5, p*=0.5. With R=1.02 ⇒ p*=0.6, price 6/1.02 = 5.88. Lesson 011's three-period tree gives 7.475 because p*=(1−0.9)/(1.1−0.9)=½ there.

Change of measure

Z = dQ/dP   ⟹   EQ[X] = EP[Z·X],   EP[Z] = 1

Same outcomes, different weights. Z(ω) is how much more (or less) Q counts outcome ω. Equivalence: Z > 0 everywhere — a measure change may re-weight outcomes but never create or destroy them.

Girsanov (constant market price of risk)

ItemValue
Weight (RN derivative)ZT = exp(−θWT − ½θ²T), and EP[ZT] = 1
New Brownian motionW̃t = Wt + θt is a BM under Q
Market price of riskθ = (μ − r)/σ (the asset's Sharpe ratio)
Stock under PdS = μS dt + σS dW
Stock under QdS = rS dt + σS dW̃, i.e. ST = S0e(r−½σ²)T + σW̃T
Discounted stockd(e−rtS) = σe−rtS dW̃ ⇒ a Q-martingale

Why only the drift moves: completing the square in the Gaussian density shows WT goes from N(0,T) to N(−θT, T) — centre shifts, width does not. Volatility is fixed by the quadratic variation [W]t = t, a path-by-path fact that equivalent measures must agree on.

Pricing

Vt = EQ[ e−r(T−t)VT | ℱt ]

Equivalently: discounted prices are Q-martingales. Replication in continuous time is guaranteed by the martingale representation theorem (Shreve II §5.3): every Q-martingale on this Brownian motion is an Itô integral, i.e. the profit of a trading strategy.

CRR tree → Black–Scholes: u = eσ√Δt, d = 1/u, p* = (erΔt − d)/(u − d); the price oscillates (odd/even n) and converges roughly like 1/n.

The two fundamental theorems

TheoremStatementConsequence
FTAP 1no arbitrage ⟺ an equivalent martingale measure Q existscan't find a Q ⇒ your model has free money (a bug)
FTAP 2completeness (everything replicable) ⟺ Q is uniquejumps / stochastic vol ⇒ many Q's ⇒ a range of prices, resolved by calibration
Traps. (1) Reading Q-probabilities as forecasts (N(d2) = 56% vs a real-world 74%). (2) Believing Girsanov can move σ — it cannot. (3) Simulating with μ instead of r in a pricer. (4) Forgetting the e−rT. (5) Thinking "risk-neutral" describes investors — the premium lives in θ. (6) Assuming Q exists/is unique (FTAP 1 / FTAP 2). (7) Breaking equivalence: Z > 0, so no measure change can make a possible crash impossible.
