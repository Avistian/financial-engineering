Long-only mid-frequency equity

Reference · the daily long-only mandate in plain words · units 171–210

Everyday picture: each afternoon you look at a list of stocks, give each a score ("how much I like this one versus the others for the next day or few"), an optimizer turns those scores into a shopping cart that is allowed only to own shares and is not allowed to grab more than a small slice of each name's daily volume, you may hold overnight, and you are judged on whether that cart beat a named index after the bill for trading.

Same long-only rules as Year 4. Different clock: hours to a few days, which Lesson 001 called mid-frequency. Years 1–4 stay required. Year 5 is extra calendar. What each unit must teach: Year 5 teaching plan. Full unit list: curriculum Year 5. The store (Year 4 Q1, units 121–130) already exists.

Words that are new on this clock

WordWhat it literally means here
Mid-frequencyHold for minutes to a few days. Not "mid-horizon" (weeks+). The Q1 lab lived here, but it was allowed to short.
ADVAverage daily dollar volume — roughly, how many dollars of this stock trade in a typical day. Your participation cap is a fraction of that.
ParticipationThe slice of today's volume you take. "3% of ADV" means you refuse to buy more than that, even if the score loves the name.
Close auctionThe end-of-day matching that sets the official close. A lot of a daily book's trading happens here.
Overnight / gapThe jump from yesterday's close to today's open. You ate that jump if you held the shares.
Open-to-closeThe return from the open print to the close print — the continuous session only.
PEADPost-earnings announcement drift: after a company reports, the price often keeps moving the same way for days. Slow digestion, not magic.
Reversal (short-horizon)Names that just fell tend, on average, to bounce a bit over the next few days — the opposite of Year 4's 12-1 momentum. The Q1 lab's rev5 is this idea.

The one pipeline Year 5 builds

liquid universe (point-in-time, ADV filter)
    → daily features (no same-day close in a close-to-close score)
    → ML ranker (walk-forward, uniqueness-weighted labels)
    → score
    → optimizer (long-only + ADV cap + impact + optional futures hedge)
    → holdings
    → hold hours to a few days
    → net IR vs the named benchmark
    → capacity vs the Year-4 book + attribution + proceed/kill

Machine learning is still the scoring step. Optimization is still the holdings step. The new load-bearing fact: if you ignore ADV and impact, the paper IR is a fiction. Year 4's monthly-tuned optimizer, run every day, goes broke (unit 191 exists to make you feel that).

How you keep score

Same IR-vs-Sharpe rule as Year 4. Tiny extra: a daily book can print a lovely gross IR and a negative net IR. The exam is net. A second extra: you may overlay a short index future so the stock cart stays long-only while the economic bet is closer to "who beats whom." Report both Sharpe (unhedged) and IR (versus the benchmark, and versus cash if you hedged).

Why Years 1–4 still come first

  • Q1 lab / 164 — you have already seen 5-day reversal as a long-short toy. Year 5 keeps the idea and drops the shorts.
  • 035 / 171 — triple-barrier labeling still applies; the barriers just sit a few days out, not a month.
  • 038–039 / 174 — daily 5-day labels overlap. Uniqueness is not optional.
  • 062–064 / 184 — impact and ADV are the binding constraint, not a footnote.
  • 121 / 141–150 — IR, long-only as a constraint, the QP. Year 5 retunes them for turnover; it does not re-derive them.
Trap. Taking Year 4's monthly optimizer and running it every close. Turnover explodes; costs eat the edge. A second trap: scoring with today's close to predict today's close-to-close return (same-day leak). A third: a beautiful IR built on names you could not have bought — they did not trade enough. Units 182, 191, and 173 exist to make those three boringly obvious.
