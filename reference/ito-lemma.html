The Itô integral

∫₀ᵗ Hs dWs = lim Σi Hti-1·(Wti − Wti-1)

Position H is taken at the left endpoint ti-1 — set before the shock (adapted / non-anticipating). Each term Hti-1·ΔWi has conditional mean 0 (known H × fresh mean-0 shock), so:

Every ∫ … dW is a martingale — it has zero drift. All average trend in Itô's lemma lives in the dt term, never the dW term.

Itô's lemma

CaseFormula
Function of Brownian motion f(W) df = f′(W)·dW + ½ f″(W)·dt
Itô process dX = a·dt + b·dW, then (dX)² = b²·dt —
Function of an Itô process f(t, X) df = ( ft + a·fx + ½ b²·fxx ) dt + b·fx·dW

Recipe: Taylor-expand → keep the one second-order term in X → substitute (dX)² = b² dt → collect dt and dW. The Itô correction is ½ b² fxx: its sign is the sign of the curvature, and it is zero for linear f (then Itô = ordinary chain rule).

Geometric Brownian motion (the workhorse model)

dS = μS·dt + σS·dW  ⟹  St = S₀·exp( (μ − ½σ²)·t + σ·Wt )
QuantityGrows atValue at t

The ½σ² gap is the volatility drag (variance drain): mean growth is μ, but the path you are likely to live through grows at μ − ½σ². At σ > √(2μ) the median growth is negative even though the mean still rises.

Traps. (1) Using the ordinary chain rule — always add ½ f″ dt unless f″ = 0. (2) log S drifts at μ − ½σ², not μ. (3) Keeping dW·dt or (dt)² — only (dW)² = dt survives. (4) Right-endpoint integrand — Itô uses the left edge; the end/midpoint is Stratonovich (no martingale, a look-ahead in finance). (5) Expecting drift in the dW term — it is always a martingale. (6) Quoting the mean as "what you'll get" — for lognormal prices mean > median.
