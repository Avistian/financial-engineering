The Black–Scholes PDE & Feynman–Kac

Reference · print-friendly cheat sheet · Units 016–020 (Year 1 Q2)

Why a differential equation and a discounted average are the same price. Taught in Lesson 016; source Shreve II Ch. 6. Builds on Lesson 015 (the $Q$-price) and Lesson 013 (Itô on a function of an Itô process).

The price as a surface

V(t, s) is the option's value when the clock says t and the share is at s. Three slopes: delta Vs (space), gamma Vss (curvature), theta Vt (time).

Itô under Q, then the hedge

dS = r S dt + σ S dW̃
dV = (Vt + r S Vs + ½ σ² S² Vss) dt + σ S Vs dW̃

Book Π = V − Vs S (long one option, short delta shares). The dW̃ cancels, and so does the r S Vs carry: dΠ = (Vt + ½ σ² S² Vss) dt. A book with no random kick is riskless.

The Black–Scholes PDE

Vt + r S Vs + ½ σ² S² Vss − r V = 0

Forced by dΠ = r Π dt (no free lunch). Equivalently: the discounted price e−rtV is a Q-martingale, so its dt coefficient is zero. The real-world drift μ never appears — it cancels in the hedge, the same way p never entered the two-state recipe.

Feynman–Kac

ut + a ux + ½ b² uxx − r u = 0,   u(T, x) = g(x)   ⟺   u(t, x) = E[ e−r(T−t) g(XT) | Xt = x ]

with dX = a dt + b dW. Specialise to a = r s, b = σ s, g(s) = (s − K)+, expectation under Q: the PDE is Lesson 015's pricing formula. Two numerical machines, one number — a tree (or a finite-difference grid) and a Monte-Carlo average must agree.

Call formula (the one case with a closed form)

C = S N(d1) − K e−rτ N(d2)

d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r + ½σ²)τ] / (σ√τ), d2 = d1 − σ√τ, τ = T − t. Delta N(d1), gamma n(d1)/(S σ √τ). The N(·) is there because a change of variables turns the PDE into the heat equation uτ = ½ σ² uxx, whose kernel is Gaussian.

Six traps

  1. μ in the PDE. Residual (μ − r) S Vs = +6.37 at the running point. A forecast, not a price.
  2. Drop ½σ²S²Vss. Ordinary calculus. Residual −3.75.
  3. Payoff as a starting condition. The PDE runs backward from date T.
  4. Drop −rV. Claiming the ticket sits on the books for free. Residual +0.52.
  5. Reading the PDE as a forecast. It uses r because it is a pricing identity.
  6. American ticket, European solver. Early exercise is a free boundary.
