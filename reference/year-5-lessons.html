Year 5 — what each lesson must teach

Units 161–200 · long-only · hours to days · teaching contract, no labs yet

Everyday picture: each afternoon you re-rank stocks for the next day or few, the optimizer may only own shares and may not take more than a slice of each name's daily volume, and you are judged on beating a named index after the trading bill. This page is what each lesson must teach. Labs are not written yet.

161 Mid-frequency vs mid-horizon: the same rules, a faster clock

Teach this one skill: Keep the long-only exam (IR vs a named benchmark) and put costs in the first sentence, not the last.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Hold is now hours to a few days. Lesson 001 called this mid-frequency. Year 4 was weeks-to-months.
  2. Same long-only rule: no borrowed-share shorts. Same exam: net IR vs a named benchmark.
  3. The Q1 lab already lived on this clock, but it was allowed to short. This year drops the shorts and adds a volume cap.
  4. Same scores, daily vs monthly rebalance, after costs: the monthly book often wins on net IR. That comparison is the opening fact.
  5. More independent bets (~2,500 days in 10 years vs ~120 months) means more power and more chances to overfit.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Taking Year 4's monthly optimizer and running it every close. Unit 181 exists to make that pain concrete; preview it here.

162 The clock of a trading day

Teach this one skill: Split a day's return into overnight, open-to-close, and the close auction, and say which one your rule is betting on.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Open: the first prints. Continuous session: the day. Close auction: the end-of-day match that sets the official close. Overnight / gap: close to next open.
  2. Heston–Korajczyk–Sadka: the cross-section is not the same at 10:00 and at 15:50.
  3. Lou–Polk–Skouras: who you hold through the close is a different bet from who you hold from open to close.
  4. A close-to-close target mixes overnight and the session. Teach when that mix is intended.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Scoring at today's close to 'predict' today's close-to-close return. That is the same-day leak (172).

163 Liquidity as a membership rule

Teach this one skill: Refuse names you cannot trade, before you compute a score.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. ADV (average daily dollar volume): roughly how many dollars of this stock trade in a typical day.
  2. Participation: the slice of today's volume you take. '3% of ADV' is a hard refusal, not a hope.
  3. Days-to-trade: how many days to enter or exit at that participation. If it is 8 days, a 1-day hold is a fantasy.
  4. Amihud as a simple illiquidity number (|return| / dollar volume). Use it as a filter, not as a premium to harvest blindly.
  5. Same signal, liquid-only vs all names: the fake IR from illiquids is the lesson.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A beautiful IR built on names that did not trade enough for your AUM.

164 Short-horizon baseline I — 1–5 day reversal

Teach this one skill: Build long-only 1–5 day reversal and net it of costs; contrast it with Year 4's 12-1.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Everyday idea: names that just fell often bounce a bit over the next few days. That is the opposite of 12-1 momentum.
  2. Jegadeesh 1990 and Lehmann 1990 are the papers. The Q1 lab's rev5 is this idea with shorts.
  3. Long-only top-decile vs long-short. Costs eat a daily reversal fast. Net IR is the only number that counts.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Calling reversal 'momentum' or running it monthly. Wrong name, wrong clock.

165 Residual and industry-neutral reversal

Teach this one skill: Strip industry and the market from the lookback so the bounce is not 'all banks fell yesterday.'

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Raw reversal buys everything that fell, including a whole industry that had a bad day.
  2. Residual reversal: bounce relative to industry (and beta). The leftover is closer to 'this name, not this sector.'
  3. Residual momentum (medium horizon) is a different cousin; name it so the labels stay clean.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A 'residual' that still contains yesterday's market because the residualization used the full sample, including today.

166 Event baseline — post-earnings announcement drift

Teach this one skill: Own names after an earnings surprise using the timestamp of the print, not the calendar date.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. PEAD: after a company reports, the price often keeps moving the same way for days. Slow digestion.
  2. Bernard–Thomas. Surprise vs a simple forecast (last year, or analysts). Teach one honest surprise definition.
  3. Announce time: after the close vs before the open. Treating an after-close print as known at that day's close is a leak.
  4. Revisions and upgrades are cousins; PEAD is the required baseline.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. An earnings calendar with a date and no time. Half the prints are after 16:00.

167 Overnight and the close auction as a holding decision

Teach this one skill: Compare hold-through-close vs flatten-at-close on the same scores.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. If you hold through the close, you eat the overnight gap (191). If you flatten, you pay two tickets (exit today, enter tomorrow) and miss the overnight return.
  2. A lot of a daily book's volume happens at the auction (188). Who you are at 15:50 is a design choice.
  3. Net IR of the two holding rules on the same ranker.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Assuming 'daily' means you are flat overnight. Many daily books are not.

168 News and text as a daily ranker, not a vibe

Teach this one skill: Turn a filing or headline into a next-day residual feature with an as-of timestamp.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Re-warm 075–076: Loughran–McDonald counts; embeddings later. The job is a rank, not a sentiment essay.
  2. Tetlock as the cautionary daily-media paper: easy to overfit.
  3. Timestamp again: a 16:01 headline is not a 15:59 feature.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A news score that uses the next morning's write-up of today's move.

169 Combining short-horizon baselines under more trials

Teach this one skill: Screen ~15 daily signals with a multiple-testing correction that matches how you will commit capital.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. More days ⇒ more trials ⇒ more false winners (007, 128). Daily does not relax the bar; it invites more searching.
  2. Bonferroni / BH on the same list. Keep only what survives the bar you would use to fund the book.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. 'We have 2,500 days so t = 2 is fine.' Breadth is not a license to skip the family of tests.

170 Q1 checkpoint — reversal + PEAD, costs first

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Liquid mask from 163. Reversal and PEAD, long-only, net IR.
  2. Same-day leak hunt on the earnings timestamps.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A checkpoint that reports gross IR.

171 The 1–5 day prediction target

Teach this one skill: Choose next-day residual, open-to-close, or a short triple-barrier, and say which clock it matches.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Three targets, same features. IC and long-only IR for each.
  2. A 5-day triple-barrier (035) is legal if the hold is 5 days. Overlap then becomes 174.
  3. Open-to-close drops the overnight. That is a different mandate (162).
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A close-to-close target built from a score that already contains today's close.

172 Point-in-time daily features

Teach this one skill: Build yesterday's residuals, volume shocks, range, overnight gap — and plant a same-day-close leak.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Allowed: information available at the decision time (e.g. yesterday's close, this morning's open if you trade at 10:00).
  2. Forbidden: today's close in a score that claims to predict today's close-to-close.
  3. Volume shocks and range are magnitude features; they can leak if they include the same interval you forecast.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. The leak is one column. The lesson plants it and requires the learner to find it.

173 IC decay over hours and days

Teach this one skill: Plot IC at close+1h, +1d, +5d and pick a hold that matches the death of the score.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Same IC idea as 133, faster grid.
  2. A score dead by tomorrow morning does not belong in a 5-day hold.
  3. This number becomes the half-life in 187.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A 5-day hold on a one-hour signal 'to reduce turnover' without checking that the IC is already gone.

174 Overlapping daily labels and uniqueness

Teach this one skill: Weight 5-day labels by how unique they are, because Tuesday's 5-day path shares days with Monday's.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Re-warm 038–039. A 5-day residual starting every day is not 2,500 independent bets.
  2. Average uniqueness. Sequential bootstrap as the sampling story.
  3. Unweighted vs uniqueness-weighted IC. The gap is the lesson.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Quoting a t-stat on overlapping 5-day returns as if n = number of rows.

175 Trees on the daily cross-section

Teach this one skill: Walk-forward a daily GBDT (regress and rank) against linear and 5-day reversal.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Same tools as 135, daily rows, uniqueness weights from 174.
  2. Purged walk-forward. No i.i.d. K-fold.
  3. Must beat or honestly lose to reversal. A tree that is reversal in costume fails 179.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A giant in-sample daily fit with 200 features and a pretty IC.

176 Short return-paths as features, without becoming DeepLOB

Teach this one skill: Compare 20-day path features to simple aggregates under an honest split, and keep tabular unless the path wins.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Re-warm 071–072 only as far as 'a sequence can be a feature.' This is not a LOB net (073).
  2. Aggregates (mean, vol, last residual) vs the raw 20-day vector.
  3. If the path does not win honestly, keep the aggregates. That decision is in-syllabus.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Importing a transformer because Year 2 Q4 exists. Deep sequence models earn their keep on microstructure and text, not by default on 20 daily returns.

177 Event-aware ML

Teach this one skill: Flag earnings and news days so the model does not treat them as ordinary Tuesdays.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A PEAD day is a different data-generating process. A flag (or a separate model) is the honest design.
  2. Compare with/without flags. Leftover PEAD after the flag is the test of whether the tree already stole 166.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Dropping earnings days from training and then trading them live.

178 Two clocks, one research book

Teach this one skill: Combine a daily ML sleeve with Year 4's monthly scores and measure incremental IR.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Grinold blend again. Correlation of the two sleeves' active returns.
  2. One long-only NAV, two rebalance clocks (234 / 198 will operationalize this).
  3. Incremental IR of adding the daily sleeve to a monthly book that already exists.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Adding the daily sleeve and quoting the combined Sharpe, which is still mostly beta.

179 When daily ML is just reversal in costume

Teach this one skill: Residualize the ML score on 5-day reversal and see whether any leftover IR remains.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Orthogonalize the score to the baseline. If leftover IR dies, the tree is a fancy reversal.
  2. Write the autopsy even if the leftover lives — say how much of the IR was the baseline.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A model card that never mentions 164.

181 Why the Year-4 optimizer, run daily, goes broke

Teach this one skill: Run the monthly-tuned QP every close and watch costs eat the book.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Take the 150 optimizer, change the calendar to daily, do not retune the turnover penalty.
  2. Turnover explodes. Net IR dies. That plot is the lesson.
  3. The fix is not 'trade less for vibes.' The fix is 182–187: ADV caps, impact, multi-period at a 1–5 day half-life.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Silently raising the turnover penalty until the plot looks like Year 4 and calling it the same model.

182 Participation caps: do not take more than a slice of ADV

Teach this one skill: Write a hard ADV constraint and list the names the optimizer wanted but could not buy.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Participation as a number (e.g. 3% of ADV) and as an inequality in the QP.
  2. Names you loved and could not own. That list is the capacity story at the name level.
  3. A soft penalty vs a hard cap. Teach both; the PM usually wants a hard cap.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A 'liquidity score' in the ranker instead of a cap in the optimizer. The ranker will still ask for 20% of a name's volume.

183 Intraday vs close-only rebalance

Teach this one skill: Compare one close optimization vs an open-and-close pair on net IR.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Close-only: one book a day, auction-heavy (188).
  2. Open + close: two decisions. More chances to be right, more tickets, more ways to leak the open print.
  3. Net IR, not 'more trades look sophisticated.'
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Using the 10:00 print in a score that was supposed to be decided at 9:31.

184 Temporary impact on a daily trade list

Teach this one skill: Apply the square-root law to today's list and subtract it from paper IR as AUM grows.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Re-warm 064 / Almgren 2005. Temporary impact grows with (your dollars / ADV)^0.5, roughly.
  2. Paper vs impact-adjusted IR. The curve vs AUM is 189's cousin.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A linear cost (spread only) at a size where impact dominates.

185 Liquidity-aware risk: exiting is the hard day

Teach this one skill: Stress a 5-day liquidation of the long-only book and watch 'diversifiers' become the same trade.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Names that look diversifying in a covariance can be the same exit when everyone sells.
  2. Kyle / Amihud again: the cost of getting out is part of risk, not a surprise.
  3. A 5-day unwind schedule at the participation cap. Days-to-exit per name.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A risk model that assumes you can trade any name tomorrow at yesterday's spread.

186 Daily futures overlay on a high-turnover stock book

Teach this one skill: Hedge beta every day with an index future while the cash names stay long-only; include roll cost.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Same idea as 153, daily. The hedge must move when the stock cart's beta moves.
  2. Unhedged Sharpe vs hedged Sharpe vs IR. Roll cost as a line item.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A hedge sized on yesterday's beta and left for a week on a book that turns over 30% a day.

Trap to teach, not a footnote. Copying Year 4's monthly half-life into the daily program.

188 Auction-aware execution

Teach this one skill: Decide how much of the list goes to the close auction vs a TWAP, and measure shortfall.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. MOC / LOC: market-on-close and limit-on-close. These are the order types a daily book actually uses (222).
  2. Perold shortfall on the daily list. Auction vs TWAP.
  3. Kissell as optional practitioner reading, not a second theory.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Assuming you get the close print as a fill. You get a fill in the auction, which can differ (162's auction jump).

189 Capacity is smaller than Year 4

What has to be taught, in order:

  2. Same names, two clocks, two curves. The comparison is the lesson.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Funding the daily book at Year 4's AUM because 'it is the same stocks.'

191 Overnight gap risk

Teach this one skill: Split P&L into overnight vs session and autopsy a gap day.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. You are long into the open if you held the close (167). One ugly gap can be the week's P&L.
  2. Overnight vs intraday contribution over a quarter. A single gap-day write-up.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Averaging overnight returns and calling the risk 'small' because the mean is small. The tail is the risk.

192 Corporate actions on a daily book

Teach this one skill: Show that missing a split mid-week lies about P&L when you rebalance every day.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. More events per hold than Year 4. The same adjuster (123 / 202) now runs every night.
  2. Miss a split on purpose; the P&L lie is larger when you trade daily.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Reusing a monthly-adjusted panel for a daily book and ignoring intra-month actions.

193 Live vs paper at this clock: stale scores and late features

Teach this one skill: Delay features by one bar and measure IR death.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A daily score that arrives at 16:20 is not a 15:50 score. A vendor blip is a missed day.
  2. Delay by one bar. The IR drop is the live-vs-paper lesson (101, again).
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A research score that uses a file that lands after the auction you claimed to trade.

194 Attribution for a daily long-only

Teach this one skill: Split a quarter of active return into selection, timing, cost, and beta.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Brinson still applies. Add a cost bucket (the bill is first-order) and a timing bucket (when you traded).
  2. Beta: the unhedged daily book still rides the tide.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Attribution that ignores costs on a 40% monthly-turnover-equivalent book.

195 Crowding: when everyone fades yesterday
