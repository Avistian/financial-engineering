Long-only mid-horizon equity

Reference · the monthly long-only mandate in plain words · units 131–170

Everyday picture: once a month you look at a list of stocks, give each a score ("how much I like this one versus the others"), an optimizer turns those scores into a shopping cart that is allowed only to own shares, you hold until next month, and you are judged on whether that cart beat a named index after costs.

This sheet is the map, not the lessons. Years 1–3 stay required. The store (Year 4 Q1, units 121–130) comes first. The monthly book is Year 4 Q2–Q4 (131–160) and is defended in Year 5 Q1 (161–170). The same long-only + ML + optimizer job on an hours-to-days clock is the daily mandate. What each unit must teach: Year 4 · Year 5 Q1.

Words (same breath as first use)

WordWhat it literally means here
Long-onlyYou only own shares. You never borrow shares to bet they fall (short).
Mid-horizonHold for weeks to a few months. Lesson 001 called this low frequency / factor investing, not "mid-frequency" (minutes–days).
BenchmarkThe comparison basket you claim to beat (often the same stocks, cap-weighted).
Active returnYour return minus the benchmark's return. The extra (or missing) piece.
Tracking errorHow much that extra piece bounces — the volatility of active return.
Information ratio (IR)Active return ÷ tracking error. "How much beat-the-index did I get per unit of being different from the index?"
Sharpe ratioExtra return over cash ÷ how hard the book bounces. A long-only book can have a fine Sharpe just by riding the market.
BetaHow hard the book rides the market tide. Unhedged long-only ≈ beta near 1.
Score / signalA number per stock today: how much you like it versus the others. ML produces scores; it does not produce holdings by itself.
OptimizerA program that turns scores into holdings under rules (no shorts, sector caps, "don't trade too much").
Information coefficient (IC)How well today's scores line up with next period's residual returns. High IC + many names = the Fundamental Law (unit 084).

The one pipeline Year 4 builds

named universe (point-in-time)
    → features (no restated numbers)
    → ML ranker (walk-forward, not i.i.d. CV)
    → score
    → constrained optimizer (long-only + risk + costs)
    → holdings
    → hold until next rebalance
    → net IR vs the named benchmark
    → capacity + attribution + proceed/kill

Machine learning is the scoring step. Optimization is the holdings step. Skipping either is a different, weaker job (a factor tilt with no model, or a model whose top-N you buy by hand).

How you keep score

Tiny numbers. Index +10%, you +12% after costs → active return = +2%. If that extra piece has bounced at 4% a year, IR ≈ 2 / 4 = 0.5. Same year, the market was +10% and you rode it: your Sharpe can look fine even if you added nothing. IR is the exam; Sharpe is the alibi.

If the index is −20% and you are −18%, you still beat it. That is the mandate, not a failed signal. A PM who forbids shorts will live through crashes with you.

The four quarters

QUnitsOne skill
Y4 Q1121–130The store: as-of keys, prices, membership, vintages, ADV, feature store. No backtest until this is green.
Y4 Q2131–140State the mandate and beat (or honestly match) classic baselines: momentum, value, quality.
Y4 Q3141–150A monthly ML ranker that respects time. Beat a linear model and 12-1 momentum, or kill the net.
Y4 Q4151–160Turn that score into a long-only book with a risk model, costs, and real constraints.
Y5 Q1161–170Run it: fills, drift, crashes, attribution, capacity. Capstone defended to a PM who only allows longs.

Why Years 1–3 still come first

  • 007 / 128 — searching many characteristics is the same multiple-testing trap with a slower clock.
  • 047 / 135 — gradient-boosted trees are the first ML ranker, not a new invention.
  • 051–060 — walk-forward / CPCV / PBO / deflated Sharpe. Monthly data gives you fewer independent bets, so the honesty bar gets stricter, not looser.
  • 081–090 — mean-variance, cleaned covariance, the Fundamental Law, costs. Year 4 Q3 is those tools with a no-short constraint bolted on.
Trap. Treating a long-only backtest's Sharpe as proof of skill. Most of that number is the market tide (beta). A second trap: leaking restated fundamentals (the company "always had" this year's earnings in the 2014 feature). A third: letting the optimizer hug the index so tracking error looks tiny and IR looks stable — you have a closet indexer, not a book. Units 123, 142, 153, and 158 exist to make those three failure modes boringly obvious.
