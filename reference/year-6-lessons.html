Year 6 — what each lesson must teach

Units 201–240 · defend the daily book, then paper and live · no labs yet

Study order is the unit numbers. Years 1–5 stay required.

201 Overnight gap risk

Teach this one skill: Split P&L into overnight vs session and autopsy a gap day.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. You are long into the open if you held the close (177). One ugly gap can be the week's P&L.
  2. Overnight vs intraday contribution over a quarter. A single gap-day write-up.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Averaging overnight returns and calling the risk 'small' because the mean is small. The tail is the risk.

202 Corporate actions on a daily book

Teach this one skill: Show that missing a split mid-week lies about P&L when you rebalance every day.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. More events per hold than Year 4. The same adjuster (133 / 122) now runs every night.
  2. Miss a split on purpose; the P&L lie is larger when you trade daily.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Reusing a monthly-adjusted panel for a daily book and ignoring intra-month actions.

203 Live vs paper at this clock: stale scores and late features

Teach this one skill: Delay features by one bar and measure IR death.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A daily score that arrives at 16:20 is not a 15:50 score. A vendor blip is a missed day.
  2. Delay by one bar. The IR drop is the live-vs-paper lesson (101, again).
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A research score that uses a file that lands after the auction you claimed to trade.

204 Attribution for a daily long-only

Teach this one skill: Split a quarter of active return into selection, timing, cost, and beta.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Brinson still applies. Add a cost bucket (the bill is first-order) and a timing bucket (when you traded).
  2. Beta: the unhedged daily book still rides the tide.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Attribution that ignores costs on a 40% monthly-turnover-equivalent book.

205 Crowding: when everyone fades yesterday

Teach this one skill: Show the post-2009 reversal fade and write the memo.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Short-horizon reversal got crowded. A rule that was easy in 1992 is a different rule now.
  2. McLean–Pontiff / Lou–Polk as the reading. The memo is the skill.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A 1990–2024 backtest whose IR is earned entirely before 2004.

206 Pre-register the daily capstone

Teach this one skill: Write kill criteria (universe, hold, ADV cap, hurdle) before the run.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Same discipline as 167, faster clock. Participation cap is a required line.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A pre-registration that omits the ADV cap.

207 Failure modes of the daily mandate

Teach this one skill: Plant and find: same-day leak, ADV-blind optimizer, closet indexer, cost-free Sharpe.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Four bugs, one notebook the learner did not write. Twin of 168.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A lecture without planted bugs.

208 Two-sleeve NAV: monthly plus daily

Teach this one skill: Put Year 4 and Year 5 on one long-only NAV; report joint IR, correlation of actives, and capacity.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. How much capital each sleeve gets. Correlation of active returns (not of scores).
  2. Joint capacity is not the sum of the two capacities if they trade the same names on the same day.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Funding both sleeves at full single-sleeve capacity.

209 Overlays: 140/30 and synthetic market-neutral

Teach this one skill: Same names, three mandates: long-only, 140/30, futures-hedged; say what IR and Sharpe each claim.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Cash stays long-only under the futures overlay (163/196). 140/30 is a different permission.
  2. Three columns of numbers. One paragraph on which mandate the PM actually gave you.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Reporting the hedged Sharpe as the long-only result.

210 Daily long-only capstone

Teach this one skill: Defend a liquid, walk-forward, ADV-and-cost-aware long-only book on net IR, with capacity vs the monthly book (170).

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. PIT liquid universe → 1–5 day ranker → ADV-capped optimizer → net IR → capacity vs Year 4 → attribution → proceed/kill.
  2. PM who forbids shorts and rebalances often.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A monthly notebook with the calendar changed to daily.

211 One code path: backtest calls the same functions as live

Teach this one skill: State the rule 'the backtest is not allowed a private score or optimizer,' and show a violation.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Everyday picture: if live calls score(as_of) and the backtest pastes a CSV of scores, they will drift apart.
  2. The backtest loops over mornings and calls the same functions live will call, with that morning's as-of store.
  3. A planted violation: a backtest-only 'adjusted' score. Catch it.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A research notebook and a 'prod script' that are cousins, not the same functions.

212 The score job: scheduled, logged, replayable

Teach this one skill: Describe a run that takes an as-of timestamp and writes scores plus a log you can replay.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Inputs: as-of morning, store revision. Outputs: score file, feature snapshot id, git hash, clock.
  2. Replay: same inputs ⇒ same scores. If not, you cannot debug Tuesday.
  3. This is not Airflow worship. It is 'write down what ran.'
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Scores that exist only in a laptop session named 'final_v3'.

213 The optimizer job: constraints as config

Teach this one skill: Put every constraint in a dated config the PM can read, not in comments in a notebook.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Sentences from 155/192 become fields: name cap, sector cap, ADV participation, turnover penalty, long-only flag.
  2. Changing a cap is a new trial (007) if you peek at IR first.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Hard-coded caps that differ between the backtest and the live job.

214 The order list a broker will accept

Teach this one skill: Turn target weights into a list of orders: name, side, quantity, type, limit, auction flag.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A broker does not take a vector of weights. It takes orders.
  2. Long-only: side is buy or sell of what you already own (no short-sell). Quantity is shares, not percent, once you pick NAV.
  3. Rounding, lot sizes, 'do not trade 12 shares of a $4 name.' Teach the boring arithmetic.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Sending weights to a broker API and hoping it guesses shares.

215 Fill model vs live fills

Teach this one skill: Keep a table of predicted fill (price, time, shortfall) next to realized fill, and read the gap.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. The backtest's fill is a model (close, VWAP, close plus a spread). Live fills are different.
  2. A standing table: date, name, predicted, realized, shortfall. This is how 162/198 stay honest after go-live.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Changing the fill model until paper matches last week's luck.

216 Position and cash ledger

Teach this one skill: Keep a book that always adds up: cash + stock market value = NAV, after every fill and dividend.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A ledger is the notebook of what you hold and how much cash you have. It is not the broker's screen — it is yours, and you will reconcile (225).
  2. Every fill, dividend, fee, and future variation-margin (if you overlay) is a row.
  3. A broken identity (cash + stock ≠ NAV) is a stop-the-line bug, not a rounding footnote.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. NAV taken from the broker and positions taken from the research file, never forced to meet.

217 Corporate actions in the live book

Teach this one skill: Apply tonight's split or dividend to positions and to open orders, not only to the research panel.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. The adjuster from 122 must run on the ledger. Share counts change. Open limit prices may need to change.
  2. A missed overnight split is a wrong order in the morning.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Adjusting research prices and leaving live share counts on the old split.

218 Calendar, holidays, half-days, auction times

Teach this one skill: Know when the market is closed, when it closes early, and when the auction is, in the timezone you trade.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A trading calendar is data. Half-days move the auction. Overseas names have other calendars if the universe ever leaves one country.
  2. A 'daily' job that runs on a holiday produces either nothing or a lie.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A cron job on weekdays that does not know about Good Friday.

219 Replay, config, and experiment ids

Teach this one skill: Reproduce Tuesday's book from config + store revision + git hash alone (unit 109, applied).

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. If you cannot replay, you cannot debug, and you cannot claim the backtest was this code.
  2. Seeds, config, store revision, code version — four fields, always written.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A live book that cannot be rebuilt from logs.

220 Q2 checkpoint — backtest and 'live' agree on one morning

Teach this one skill: Pick an as-of date; the backtest path and the live job path produce the same scores, weights, and orders.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Same functions. Same store revision. Byte-for-byte or a stated rounding rule.
  2. A planted private backtest path must fail the test.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A checkpoint that compares IR over a year instead of the objects on one morning.

221 Broker mechanics for a long-only cash book

Teach this one skill: Explain account, buying power, cash, and why you do not need a stock borrow — and what still can reject an order.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A broker is the firm that holds your cash and sends your orders to the exchange.
  2. Buying power: how many dollars you may still spend. Long-only cash: you cannot buy more than cash (plus any agreed margin — teach the default as cash-only).
  3. No short ⇒ no locate. You can still be rejected: halted name, not enough cash, restricted list (228), odd lots.
  4. Paper account vs live account: same objects, fake money. Teach the objects before any API brand.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Assuming 'long-only' means every order is accepted.

222 Order types you will actually use

Teach this one skill: Define market, limit, market-on-close, and limit-on-close, and say which one a monthly vs daily book uses.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Market: take whatever price is there. Fast, sloppy.
  2. Limit: do not pay worse than this price. You may not fill.
  3. MOC / LOC: participate in the close auction, with or without a limit.
  4. Year 4 monthly lists often TWAP over days (093) then finish in auctions. Year 5 lives on MOC/LOC. Teach both maps.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A backtest that assumes market orders at the close print.

223 How the close auction works, operationally

Teach this one skill: Walk an order from 'I want this many shares at the close' to a fill, including imbalance and why you might not get the print.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Imbalance: more buy interest than sell interest (or the reverse) going into the auction. The print can move.
  2. Cutoff times. A late MOC is a missed trade (203).
  3. This is 198 as operations, not as a shortfall formula.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Treating the official close as a guaranteed fill price decided at 15:00.

224 The paper-trading loop

Teach this one skill: Write the daily loop in words and objects: as-of store → score → optimize → orders → fills → ledger → NAV.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Paper trading is doing the whole loop with fake money and (ideally) real or delayed market data.
  2. Each arrow is a function from 211–216. The loop is the product.
  3. A week of paper is 230. This lesson teaches the loop, not the week.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Calling a backtest 'paper trading.' A backtest can peek by construction; paper cannot.

225 Reconciliation: your ledger vs the broker

Teach this one skill: Every day, match positions, cash, and fills; a break is a halt, not a footnote.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Three files: your ledger, the broker's positions, the day's fills. They must tell the same story.
  2. Breaks: missed fill, duplicate fill, corporate action only on one side, timezone.
  3. The rule: do not send new orders until yesterday reconciles.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Reconciling once a month on a daily book.

226 Kill switch and risk limits

Teach this one skill: Write the limits as sentences and the kill as a single action that flattens or freezes.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Limits: max name, max sector, max gross, min cash, max daily loss, max tracking error, max participation.
  2. A kill switch is the button that stops new risk: freeze orders, or flatten to cash / to the benchmark. Teach both flavors and when each is appropriate for long-only.
  3. A limit breach is not 'log and continue.' It is refuse or kill.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Limits that exist in a slide deck and not in the order path.

227 Slippage tracking: predicted vs realized

Teach this one skill: Read the 215 table every week and decide whether the fill model is a lie.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Slippage is implementation shortfall per name per day. Predicted vs realized.
  2. A model that is always optimistic will make every paper IR a fiction (162).
  3. Feed the gap back into 156/194. That loop is research, not operations theater.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Resetting the fill model every time last week was unlucky.

228 Restricted lists and compliance holds

Teach this one skill: Treat a banned name as a hard zero in the optimizer and as a reject at the broker.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Client bans, insider lists, ESG screens, sanctions. The research universe and the live order path must both see the list.
  2. A name added to the list mid-day: you may not buy more; you may have to exit. Teach the two cases.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Filtering restricted names in the score and not in the optimizer (or the reverse).

229 The shape of the trading day

Teach this one skill: Write the runbook times: pre-open checks, decision time, auction cutoff, overnight adjust, reconcile.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Year 4: a quiet month, then a rebalance window of a few days.
  2. Year 5: a clock. Feature ready by T1, scores by T2, orders by T3, auction cutoff T4, ledger T5.
  3. Holidays from 218 sit on this clock.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A 'daily job' with no cutoff times.

230 Q3 checkpoint — a reconciled week of paper

Teach this one skill: Five paper days: loop runs, ledger equals broker, a kill-switch test fires on a planted breach, no silent rejects.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Reconcile each day (225). One planted limit breach must kill or refuse (226).
  2. A written shortfall table (227) even if the week is tiny.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Five backtest days labeled 'paper.'

231 Going live small

Teach this one skill: State the first-week rules: tiny AUM, few names, one sleeve, a written abort.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Small means a size where a mistake is tuition, not a career event. Name a dollar number in the lesson as a worked example, not as advice.
  2. One sleeve first (monthly or daily, not both). Restricted list on. Kill switch tested in paper (230) before live.
  3. Abort: what happens if reconcile fails on day two.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Going live at research AUM because the paper IR was pretty.

232 Monitoring expected vs realized

Teach this one skill: Each week: IR, turnover, rejects, shortfall, beta — versus what the backtest promised for this kind of week.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A dashboard is a list of comparisons, not a pile of charts. Teach the list.
  2. Rejects and unfilled MOCs are first-class. They change the book you actually had.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Watching NAV and ignoring rejects.

233 Incident log

Teach this one skill: Write one page per incident: missed auction, stale feature, wrong split, vendor hole — cause, P&L, fix, test.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. If it is not written, it will recur. This is the operational twin of a research autopsy (149).
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Fixing the file by hand and not adding a test to 130/220.

234 Two-frequency operations

Teach this one skill: Run a monthly sleeve and a daily sleeve on one NAV without double-counting ADV or cash.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Unit 208 as operations: two clocks, one ledger (216), one restricted list (228), one kill switch (226).
  2. ADV is shared. If both sleeves buy AAA on the same day, participation adds. Teach the combined cap.
  3. Cash is shared. A monthly rebalance week plus a daily book can overdraw buying power (221).
  4. Correlation of active returns is a research number; the operational number is 'did we exceed ADV today?'
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Funding both sleeves at full single-sleeve participation.

235 Vendor outage and fallback

Teach this one skill: Write what you do when the store does not land: skip, flatten, or use a stale-but-labeled snapshot — never a silent guess.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A missing as-of table is a halt condition for new risk (226). You may hold what you have.
  2. A fallback snapshot must be labeled stale in the log (212 / 219). The backtest is not allowed to invent that morning later.
  3. Which fields are required vs optional (prices required; a news feature can be missing).
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Filling a hole with yesterday's scores and not writing it down.

236 Tax lots and after-tax as an overlay (awareness)

Teach this one skill: Name what a tax lot is, and why it must not rewrite the pre-tax research book.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. A lot is a bundle of shares you bought on a given day at a given price. Selling can be 'oldest first' or 'highest cost first.'
  2. After-tax P&L is a different exam. Teach it as an overlay after the pre-tax capstones (170 / 210) are honest.
  3. Do not let tax rules silently change the research target.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. Optimizing the research book for a tax story you have not pre-registered.

237 Pre-register the live trial

Teach this one skill: Write duration, AUM, abort rules, and what would count as 'paper was a lie' before the first live order.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Twin of 167/206, now with broker and reconcile. Duration (e.g. 20 days), AUM, sleeve, abort (225/226), success definition.
  2. If live shortfall is 3× the model, that is a kill of the fill model, not a nudge of the score.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A live trial whose success definition is 'NAV went up.'

238 Failure modes of going live

Teach this one skill: Plant and find: silent stale scores, unreconciled ledger, limits not on the order path, a second sleeve added on day three.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Four operational bugs. The learner finds them in a runbook they did not write.
  2. This is 168/207 for the desk, not for the ranker.
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A 'go-live risks' slide with no planted break.

239 The runbook

Teach this one skill: Write the one document a tired person can follow: times, commands, who to call, what 'halt' means.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. Contents: clock (229), reconcile (225), kill (226), outage (235), incidents (233), two-sleeve notes (234).
  2. If it is not in the runbook, it will be improvised. Improvisation is a failure mode (238).
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A runbook that is a copy of the research memo.

240 Year-6 exit — a desk you could actually run

Teach this one skill: Defend 20 paper (or tiny live) days: store passes 130, backtest=live on a morning (220), week reconciles (230), runbook, proceed/kill.

What has to be taught, in order:

  1. This capstone is operations, not a prettier IR. The Year-4 and Year-5 books are inputs.
  2. Required: leak tests green, one replayed morning, a reconciled stretch, a kill-switch test, a shortfall table, a written abort/proceed.
  3. PM who forbids shorts and asks 'can we turn this on Monday?'
Trap to teach, not a footnote. A capstone that is another backtest with a 'we would paper trade' paragraph.
